A PRACTICAL START

How to get started with NotebookLM.

  1. Create a notebook and add relevant sources supported by the current interface.
  2. Ask specific questions about the supplied material and inspect citations or source references.
  3. Use the output to build study notes or an outline, then confirm critical details in the original documents.
CHECK BEFORE YOU COMMIT

Important limits to check.

  • Supported source types, limits, and generated features can change by account and region.
  • Source-grounded answers can still misread or omit context; check the original passage.
  • Do not upload confidential or sensitive documents without checking permissions and privacy requirements.

PRACTICAL EVALUATION GUIDE

How to use NotebookLM with care.

Who it may suit: Students organising class materials; researchers reviewing a defined set of sources; teams preparing summaries from approved internal documents. This is an independent orientation guide, not an endorsement or benchmark. Google's official documentation is the authority for current capabilities, supported regions, plan limits, privacy settings and terms. Start with a small, low-risk test and compare the result with your existing workflow. Measure the time spent checking and correcting the output, not only the speed of the initial response.

Three concrete use cases

1) Add a small set of trusted sources and ask for a topic summary. 2) Create study questions from supplied notes and compare them with the original material. 3) Identify where sources disagree before drafting a briefing.

Potential advantages

Working from a chosen source set can help organise notes and study. It is useful for question-led exploration of a bounded collection of documents. For a fair comparison, use the same brief, context and acceptance criteria across tools. Track whether the tool improves the final result, reduces repetitive work or makes a task easier for your team. A fast draft is not automatically a finished deliverable.

Limitations and trade-offs

It can miss context or misattribute details; source selection determines quality; supported formats, limits and privacy terms should be checked. Keep human review in the workflow. Check accuracy, context, permissions, accessibility and technical quality before relying on the result. Generated code, summaries or recommendations can be plausible and still be wrong.

Two alternatives to compare

Compare ChatGPT and Perplexity as well as your current workflow. Alternatives can differ in integrations, supported formats, collaboration, learning curve and cost. AI FACTORY does not claim one provider is universally best. Verify current quotas, billing currency, taxes, cancellation terms and commercial rights directly with each provider before paying.

Who should not rely on it alone

NotebookLM may not be suitable as the only system for work requiring guaranteed accuracy, regulated professional judgement, confidential data without approved safeguards or a legally binding decision. It may also be a poor fit if you need predictable results without review, offline operation, a specific integration that has not been confirmed, or rights the provider does not clearly grant. Use a qualified professional or approved workflow when those requirements apply.

Privacy, rights and buying checklist

Before uploading customer information, private documents, proprietary material or source code, review the provider's privacy policy and terms. Check retention, training controls, access permissions, deletion options and consent requirements. Use only material you have permission to process. If considering a paid plan, verify live pricing, usage limits, commercial-use conditions and renewal details on the official checkout page. AI FACTORY does not guarantee fixed prices. Test with non-sensitive material and decide whether the measured benefit justifies the cost.
